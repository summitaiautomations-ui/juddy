1

Import Local Media

  • Keep .insv pairs and all chunks together in one folder — Studio stitches them. Same date+time prefix = one recording.
  • .lrv files are camera proxies. Fine for previewing, never the export source.
  • "Some footage cannot be found" = a clip moved since last open. Put it back or re-import.
2

Scrub & find the shot

  • Drag the white dot on the yellow bar under the preview — it spans the whole clip, so small drags jump far.
  • Timeline too tight? Hit the − magnifier (bottom right) so the full clip fits, then drag the playhead.
  • No fast-forward playback exists — dragging is the move.
3

Reframe Video panel

  • Zoom out: drag the FOV number (top-left value) up, or tap a View Angle preset — ULTRA / MEGA for wide.
  • Level it: drag the preview to fix tilt (pitch) and direction (yaw). A big negative pitch = staring at the water.
  • Moving subject: set a keyframe, move the playhead, re-aim, set again. Or hit Deep Track and let it follow.
FRAMING RULE · face in, shoreline & pond out.
4

Pick the aspect before export

  • Tap the 16:9 icon under the preview and choose 16:9 or 9:16.
  • Reframe each one separately. A vertical crop needs a different aim and FOV than the horizontal — don't reuse the same keyframes.
  • Export horizontal first, switch to 9:16, re-aim, export again. Two files, one clip.
5

Export × 2

  • Export as: Video (not 360 Video — that's the unreframed sphere).
  • Name them so FCP tells them apart: …_H.mp4 and …_V.mp4.
  • Path → the external edit drive, in the day's folder.
  • Check the Resolution box — it's the proof of which aspect you're in.
  • Expect ~5–6 GB per 12 min at 4K. Normal.

6

Final Cut Pro

  • One project per aspect. Horizontal: 3840×2160. Vertical: Custom 2160×3840. Frame rate = match the export.
  • Import both files → Spatial Conform Fill so the clip fills the frame with no bars.
  • Cut first, polish second. Make all your editorial cuts on the long clip, then tweak per shot — far less keyframing.
  • Need to punch in? 4K gives you room — stay under ~120% scale on a 1080/1920 delivery and it still looks clean.
Zoomed way in?FOV in the 40s means a tiny slice of the sphere. Push it past 100° and re-aim.
Sluggish timeline?Export H.264 instead of H.265 — same look, snappier scrubbing in FCP.
Wrong aspect exported?Don't crop in FCP. Go back to Studio, switch to 9:16, export again.
Chunks missing?Never separate the numbered .insv pieces. Studio needs every chunk to build the master.
